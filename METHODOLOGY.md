# WHAT WOULD YOU DO? — Season 1 Methodology

## Purpose

WHAT WOULD YOU DO? is an educational decision game about gender-based violence and related questions of consent, boundaries, safety, evidence, dignity, respect and responsible action.

It is designed for six different audiences because age and sex can shape the situations people encounter, the pressures they face and the safest way educational material should be presented.

The six paths use the same underlying rights standard. They do not assume that all men are perpetrators, all women are victims, or that every person in an age group has the same experience.

## Season 1 structure

Each path contains 200 playable decisions across five stages:

- Stage 1: 20
- Stage 2: 30
- Stage 3: 40
- Stage 4: 50
- Stage 5: 60

A player needs 70% to pass a stage.

Across all six paths, Season 1 contains 1,200 playable decisions.

## The six learning frameworks

### Women 18+

Focus areas include coercive control, consent, intimate-partner violence, economic abuse, stalking, workplace harassment, technology-facilitated abuse, family and marriage pressure, survivor support, bystander choices, evidence, disability-related power imbalances and migration-related retaliation.

The game must never suggest that avoiding violence is primarily a woman's responsibility.

### Men 18+

Focus areas include respect, consent, jealousy, surveillance, sexual entitlement, peer norms, anger and intimidation, accountability between men, workplace power, fatherhood, safe bystander action, support for survivors and male victimization.

The path is not an accusation exercise. It asks men to consider themselves as partners, friends, fathers, colleagues, bystanders, survivors and people responsible for their own behaviour.

### Girls 13–17

Focus areas include first relationships, consent, pressure, sexualized rumours, intimate-image abuse, school harassment, controlling dating behaviour, online grooming, adult boundary violations, friendships, witnessing violence at home and safe help-seeking.

Teen players are not asked to investigate abuse or manage dangerous adults.

### Boys 13–17

Focus areas include respectful relationships, consent, peer pressure, online image-sharing, misogynistic group norms, jealousy, anger, bystander choices, adult boundary violations, male victimization and challenging harmful ideas about masculinity.

The game avoids treating masculinity itself as a problem. It focuses on concrete choices and social pressure.

### Girls 8–12

Focus areas include body autonomy, saying no to unwanted touch, safe and unsafe secrets, gifts used to manipulate, online safety, bullying, trusted adults, helping friends and what to do when adults at home behave violently.

### Boys 8–12

Focus areas include body boundaries, respecting another child's "stop", unsafe secrets, online manipulation, bullying, pressure to fight, emotional stereotypes, trusted adults, helping friends and the principle that boys also have a right to protection.

## Child safeguarding rules

For the 8–12 paths:

- no graphic descriptions of sexual or physical violence;
- no adult relationship disputes presented as the child's problem;
- no instruction to investigate suspected abuse;
- no instruction to confront a dangerous adult;
- no suggestion that a child must physically stop adult violence;
- no use of fear as entertainment;
- no collection of full name, school, home address, date of birth or email;
- repeat the principle that if one safe adult does not listen, the child can tell another.

For 13–17 paths, sexual and relationship material is kept age-appropriate and is presented through boundaries, pressure, safety, respect and help-seeking rather than explicit adult sexual content.

## Editorial rules

Every scenario should:

1. give the player a real decision rather than an obvious joke answer;
2. distinguish a warning sign from proof of a legal offence;
3. distinguish an allegation from an established fact;
4. avoid survivor blame;
5. avoid vigilantism;
6. avoid assuming police are universally safe or accessible;
7. avoid applying one country's law across all African contexts;
8. avoid suggesting confrontation when it could raise risk;
9. protect privacy;
10. explain why the strongest option is stronger.

## What the game scores

The game tracks seven decision skills:

- Awareness
- Empathy
- Safety
- Rights
- Evidence
- Judgement
- Courage

The score describes performance in the game. It is not a moral rating of the player.

## Feedback model

Each answered decision gives:

**YOUR CHOICE → WHAT HAPPENS → WHY IT MATTERS → WHAT TO REMEMBER → LEARN MORE**

Wrong answers are framed as learning opportunities, not as personal failure.

## African context

The game uses fictional settings and characters across Cameroon, Nigeria, Ghana, Kenya, South Africa, Uganda, Zambia, Namibia, Zimbabwe, Senegal and Morocco.

Settings include homes, schools, universities, workplaces, public transport, family gatherings, sport, community events, social media, WhatsApp groups, gaming spaces and migration-related situations.

African context should be ordinary and specific, not a shorthand for violence or cultural backwardness.

## Evidence base

Season 1 draws its core educational principles from:

- World Health Organization — violence against women
- World Health Organization — violence against children
- WHO INSPIRE violence-prevention framework
- UNICEF — violence against children
- UNICEF — keeping children safe online
- UNICEF — child-friendly Convention on the Rights of the Child
- UN Women — prevention of violence against women
- UN Women — technology-facilitated violence and online safety
- UN Women — engaging boys and young men in gender equality
- UNFPA — gender-based violence and survivor-centred support

Each playable decision links to a relevant source through **LEARN MORE**.

## Privacy

Progress is stored in the browser using local storage.

Each path has its own profile and progress state so one household can use the same device for several different paths.

Under-18 paths ask only for a badge name or nickname. No email is requested.

## Review standard

Before a scenario is accepted, it should pass five questions:

- Is it realistic?
- Is there actually something to think about?
- Is the explanation defensible?
- Is it appropriate for this age and path?
- Would the player remember something useful from it tomorrow?

## Status

This methodology governs Season 1. Future seasons should preserve the safeguarding rules while expanding countries, recurring story arcs, institutional settings and reviewed source material.
