# WHAT WOULD YOU DO? — Engagement & Motion Master Prompt

## Goal

Make the game feel alive, polished and memorable without turning gender-based violence into entertainment.

Engagement should come from **decision-making, story movement, visual feedback, sound, progress and discovery** — never from making violence itself exciting.

## Non-negotiable rules

1. Every sound must be optional and easy to mute.
2. No harsh “wrong answer” buzzers, alarm sounds or shame cues.
3. No violent sound effects.
4. No continuous background music by default.
5. Motion must respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and include an in-game motion control.
6. Children get more playful motion; teens get brisker motion; adults get calmer, more restrained motion.
7. Male and female paths may have different palettes and sonic signatures, but avoid crude gender stereotypes.
8. Feedback should reward thoughtful decisions, not speed.
9. Animation must never delay access to the next action.
10. The game must remain usable with sound and motion completely off.

## Demographic experience systems

### Women 18+
Warm plum / burgundy visual system. Calm, polished transitions. Warm, soft tonal cues. Minimal celebratory sparkles.

### Men 18+
Forest / teal visual system. Grounded, clean movement. Lower, warm tonal cues. Strong but restrained stage-completion animation.

### Girls 13–17
Violet / berry system. Brisk transitions, energetic cards and lively but respectful progress motion. Bright, clean tonal cues.

### Boys 13–17
Indigo / cobalt system. Crisp movement, quick progress pulses and energetic stage unlocks. Clear rhythmic tonal cues.

### Girls 8–12
Berry / cream system. Friendly bounce, rounded motion, soft star/circle celebrations and gentle bell-like cues.

### Boys 8–12
Turquoise / teal / warm-gold system. Friendly spring motion, simple shape celebrations and bright, gentle cues.

## Sound language

Use Web Audio generated in-browser so the game works offline and does not depend on external audio files.

Create cues for:
- option selected;
- strong choice;
- learning moment;
- next decision;
- stage passed;
- stage retry;
- badge earned;
- Season 1 completed.

The “learning moment” cue must be neutral and encouraging — never a failure buzzer.

## Motion language

Use:
- screen entrance transitions;
- question-card reveal;
- answer selection pulse;
- progress-bar movement;
- feedback reveal;
- checkpoint moments;
- stage unlock animation;
- badge shine;
- subtle celebratory particles for passed stages;
- a larger but still dignified Season 1 celebration.

## Extra engagement

Add:
- quarter / halfway / three-quarter stage checkpoints;
- streak feedback that celebrates consistency without encouraging speed;
- demographic-specific microcopy;
- visible sound and motion controls in the game header;
- persistent settings stored locally;
- optional mobile haptic taps where supported;
- no timers and no speed scoring.

## Standard

The experience should feel like a professionally designed narrative decision game — not an online quiz and not a classroom worksheet.
