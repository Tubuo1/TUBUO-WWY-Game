# WHAT WOULD YOU DO? — Master Content Prompt

## Objective

Rebuild and continuously review every question in **WHAT WOULD YOU DO?** so the game is genuinely absorbing, reader-friendly, age-appropriate and memorable while maintaining rigorous safeguarding and GBV education.

The non-negotiable rule is:

> **No question may appear twice. No repeated scenario with a different name. No reused question disguised by changing a city, pronoun, character or a few words.**

A recurring topic may return because learning needs reinforcement, but the **situation, decision, social context, stakes, wording and teaching angle must be meaningfully different**.

## Season 1 structure

Maintain all six game paths:

- Women 18+
- Men 18+
- Girls 13–17
- Boys 13–17
- Girls 8–12
- Boys 8–12

Each path contains exactly 200 questions in five stages:

- Stage 1 — 20
- Stage 2 — 30
- Stage 3 — 40
- Stage 4 — 50
- Stage 5 — 60

Season 1 therefore contains exactly **1,200 playable questions**.

## Zero-repetition standard

For every question, verify uniqueness at four levels:

1. **Story uniqueness** — the same event must not reappear later with only names, sex, city or platform changed.
2. **Decision uniqueness** — the player must not repeatedly be asked to make the same decision in essentially the same circumstances.
3. **Language uniqueness** — avoid recurring formulaic openings, question stems and answer sets.
4. **Feedback uniqueness** — explanations should respond to the actual situation instead of repeating stock text.

A concept such as consent, coercive control, bystander action or online abuse may recur only through a genuinely new situation or perspective.

## Engagement standard

Every question should feel like a small scene rather than a textbook item.

Use ordinary, believable moments: voice notes, family dinners, school corridors, football practice, bus rides, workplaces, weddings, group chats, gaming, first dates, community events, university life, neighbourhood conversations, public transport, shops and social media.

Vary the format naturally:

- What would you do?
- Spot the red flag
- The group chat
- A friend tells you…
- One minute to decide
- What changed?
- What would you say?
- Evidence check
- Myth or reality
- The missing detail
- Bystander moment
- Boundary check
- New information
- Boss case

Do not manufacture excitement around violence. The **decision-making** should be engaging; suffering must never become entertainment.

## Reader-friendliness

Write like a thoughtful human speaking plainly.

- Short sentences where possible.
- Natural dialogue.
- No NGO jargon.
- No lecture tone.
- No artificial-sounding transitions.
- No inflated language.
- No unnecessary legal terminology.
- Explain unfamiliar terms in the glossary.
- Keep answer choices plausible enough that the player has to think.
- Wrong options should reflect mistakes real people could make, not absurd answers included only to make the correct response obvious.

## Age adaptation

### Ages 8–12

Questions should be short, concrete and reassuring. Focus on boundaries, safe adults, unsafe secrets, bullying, online contact, uncomfortable behaviour and helping a friend safely.

Never ask a child to investigate suspected abuse, physically intervene in adult violence, confront a dangerous adult or decide whether an abuse allegation is legally proven.

### Ages 13–17

Use school, friendships, sports, first relationships, social media, peer pressure, rumours, image sharing, trusted adults and digital life. Do not write explicit adult sexual content.

### Adults 18+

Use realistic relationship, family, workplace, public-space, digital, financial and community situations. Include ambiguity, power imbalances and difficult bystander decisions without making the player guess obscure law.

## Male and female paths

Do not create mirrored versions.

Male paths should include responsibility, consent, peer norms, safe intervention, supporting survivors and male victimization without treating men and boys collectively as perpetrators.

Female paths should include agency, boundaries, support, work, relationships, digital life and survivor-centred decisions without implying women and girls are responsible for preventing violence committed against them.

## African reality

Use African settings as ordinary lived environments, not as decoration and not as shorthand for crisis.

Represent cities, towns, schools, homes, families, work, public transport, sports, faith/community settings and digital spaces across the countries represented in TUBUO Writes. Avoid caricatures and sweeping cultural claims.

## Question architecture

Each production question should carry:

- unique question ID;
- demographic path;
- stage;
- country/place/context;
- scenario format;
- topic;
- skill;
- unique story signature;
- question text;
- four plausible choices;
- strongest supported choice;
- immediate consequence;
- explanation;
- one memorable takeaway;
- credible source;
- safeguarding review status.

## Quality gate

Reject a question unless all are true:

- It has not appeared before in substance or language.
- A real person could plausibly face the situation.
- At least two answer choices require thought.
- The strongest answer is defensible.
- The explanation teaches something useful.
- The wording fits the player’s age.
- The question does not blame a survivor.
- It does not reward unsafe confrontation.
- It distinguishes allegation from established fact where relevant.
- It would still be worth remembering tomorrow.

## Automated QA

Before release, run a global check across all 1,200 questions for:

- exact duplicate question text;
- duplicate story IDs/signatures;
- duplicate answer sets;
- near-duplicate question wording;
- repeated base scenario within a path;
- wrong stage counts;
- missing sources;
- missing feedback;
- invalid answer keys.

A build must fail rather than publish if an exact duplicate question or story signature is detected.

## Final standard

The player should never feel that they are completing 200 versions of the same quiz.

They should feel that each tap opens a **new human situation**, with a new decision worth thinking about.
